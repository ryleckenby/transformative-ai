# Discovery Question Bank

Questions for the first conversations with a team you might enable or embed with. Organized by what you're trying to learn, not by meeting agenda — pull what you need. The meta-rule: **ask about the work, not about AI.** "Where could you use AI?" gets you their guesses about technology; "walk me through yesterday" gets you the truth about the work.

## Qualifying the team (is this the right engagement?)

- Who is asking for this — the people who do the work, their leader, or someone above both? *(You want at least the leader; ideally all three.)*
- If this works, what will you do with the capacity it frees? *(No answer → the win has no owner. Concrete answer → real pull.)*
- Who on your team would you give a few hours a week to make this work? *(Names a champion, or reveals there isn't one.)*
- What's the last process change your team actually adopted? How did it go? *(Adoption track record predicts adoption.)*
- Who controls the systems and data this workflow touches, and what's your relationship with them like? *(Access is the #1 schedule killer — scout it now.)*

## Finding the workflow (where's the leverage?)

- Walk me through yesterday. Not a typical day — literally yesterday.
- What do you do every single day/week that you wish you never had to do again?
- What work do you *not* do that you know you should? *(The invisible backlog — often the biggest win, because coverage gains don't threaten anyone's job.)*
- Where does work pile up? What's the queue everyone complains about?
- What does a new hire take the longest to get good at? *(Locates the judgment-with-pattern work — prime AI territory.)*
- If you had one extra experienced person on the team, what would you have them do?
- What do you produce that someone else then checks? What do you check that someone else produces? *(Maps the draft/verify pairs — the natural human-AI seams.)*

## Understanding one workflow deeply (once you've picked it)

- Show me the last three real examples — the actual tickets/documents/requests, not sanitized ones.
- What arrives, and what's wrong with how it arrives?
- At this step, what are you actually deciding? What would make you decide differently? *(Separates mechanical from judgment.)*
- What's the workaround here? What does the official process say, and what do you actually do? *(The real workflow lives in the gap.)*
- How do you know when this output is good? Who complains when it's bad, and how fast?
- What's the worst mistake someone could make in this workflow? Has it happened? *(Failure cost calibrates how much autonomy to design in.)*
- What knowledge do you use here that isn't written down anywhere? *(Tribal knowledge = context the AI will need = documents to create.)*
- How long does one unit take, end to end? How much of that is waiting? *(Cycle time often hides in handoffs, not work — sometimes the AI win is in the handoff.)*

## Baseline and measurement (before you build anything)

- How many of these per week? What's the range — worst week, best week?
- What do you currently measure about this work, and where does that number live?
- If we cut the time in half but errors went up a little, is that a good trade here — or is this a zero-mistakes workflow? *(Forces the speed/quality priority into the open.)*
- Four weeks from now, what number would convince *you* — not your boss — that this worked?

## Trust and people (will this survive contact with the team?)

- Has anyone here tried using AI on this already? What happened? *(Shadow usage = free pilot data; a prior failure = objections you must answer.)*
- What's the team's biggest worry about this — honestly?
- Whose job changes most if this works? Have they been in these conversations? *(If no: stop and fix that before building anything.)*
- Who's the informal expert everyone actually asks? *(That person's buy-in decides the pilot. Recruit them or fail.)*

## Closing every discovery conversation

- What should I have asked that I didn't?
- Who else should I talk to — especially someone who'll disagree with everything you just told me?
