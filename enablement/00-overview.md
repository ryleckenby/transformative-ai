# AI Enablement: An Overview

## The problem enablement exists to solve

Every wave of general-purpose technology has had an adoption gap: the lag between capability existing and organizations restructuring around it. Electricity took roughly 30 years to transform factories, because the win wasn't swapping steam engines for motors; it was redesigning the factory floor around distributed power. AI is following the same curve, compressed.

The symptom today: an organization buys AI tooling, announces a strategy, runs a lunch-and-learn, and six months later usage is a thin layer of email drafting and meeting summaries. The capability is transformative; the deployment is cosmetic.

**AI enablement is the function that closes this gap.** It is deliberately not:

- **IT provisioning.** Getting people access is table stakes, not enablement.
- **Training** in the classroom sense. Courses transfer facts, not practice.
- **Governance.** Necessary guardrails, but guardrails don't create motion.
- **Evangelism.** Enthusiasm without workflow integration decays in weeks.

Enablement is closer to a craft apprenticeship run at organizational scale: pairing people who understand the capability with people who own real work, until the work itself changes shape.

## Three levels of enablement

It helps to be precise about what level you're operating at, because the tactics are different:

### Level 1: Individual augmentation
Helping individuals use AI in their existing tasks. Prompting skills, tool fluency, knowing what AI is good and bad at. High reach, shallow impact. This is where most corporate programs stop, and why most corporate programs disappoint.

### Level 2: Workflow redesign
Taking a specific recurring workflow (triaging support tickets, drafting contract redlines, producing a weekly ops report) and rebuilding it with AI in the loop. This requires someone to sit with the team, map the workflow, identify where judgment is truly needed versus where it's habit, and build the new path. Medium reach, deep impact. **This is where transformation actually happens**, and it's the level both enablement teams and forward-deployed engineers primarily operate at.

### Level 3: Operating model change
Restructuring what the organization does because AI changed the economics: collapsing handoffs, merging roles, offering services previously uneconomical. This is executive-led and emerges from a critical mass of Level 2 wins, not from strategy decks.

## The enablement function

A serious enablement function combines four ingredients:

1. **Embedded practitioners**: people who ship alongside the teams they enable (see [forward deployment](../forward-deployment/00-overview.md) for the strongest version of this pattern).
2. **A champions network**: early adopters inside each team, given time, support, and status for spreading practice locally.
3. **Shared infrastructure**: vetted tools, reusable patterns, prompt/agent libraries, evaluation harnesses, so every team isn't solving the same problems from scratch.
4. **Measurement**: see [04-measuring-impact.md](04-measuring-impact.md). Without it you cannot distinguish adoption from adoption theater.
